What is a limit order on Polymarket?

A limit order lets you name your price instead of taking whatever the book offers right now. The cost is that it may never fill.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

A limit order on Polymarket is an order with a price cap when you buy, or a price floor when you sell. If the order book already has a matching offer, it fills right away. If not, it waits in the book until another trader takes it, it expires, or you cancel it. An order that waits and gets filled is a maker order, and Polymarket charges makers no fee.

The short answer

A limit order says: buy at this price or lower, or sell at this price or higher. Polymarket's documentation puts it simply: use one "when price control matters more than immediate execution."

What happens next depends on the book:

  • If someone is already selling at your price or better, your order fills right away, like a market order. That part pays the normal taker fee.
  • If nobody is, your order rests in the book and waits. It can fill all at once, in pieces as different traders take it, or not at all.

A market order is the opposite: it fills now against the best offers available, and any part that can't fill is cancelled. It never waits.

How long it waits

Polymarket has a few options for how long a limit order stays open:

  • GTC (good till cancelled): stays until it fills or you cancel it.
  • GTD (good till date): stays until a time you choose. The expiry must be at least 3 minutes ahead.
  • Post-only: if the order would fill right away, it is rejected instead. This makes sure you only ever add to the book, never take from it.

Your price must follow the market's tick size, the smallest price step it accepts, and your size must meet the market's minimum number of shares. See the minimum bet on Polymarket.

On sports markets, Polymarket automatically cancels open limit orders when the game begins. Start times can shift, so the docs advise watching your orders around kickoff.

Why use one

Worked example

On a politics market, Yes has a best bid of 34¢ and a best ask of 40¢. You want 100 shares.

Buy now: you pay the ask. 100 × $0.40 = $40, plus a taker fee of 100 × 0.04 × 0.40 × 0.60 = $0.96. Total about $40.96.

Limit buy at 35¢: your order becomes the new best bid and waits. If a seller fills it, you pay 100 × $0.35 = $35 and no Polymarket fee, because you were the maker.

Difference: $40.96 − $35 = $5.96, if it fills. If the price climbs to 50¢ without ever coming back to 35¢, you get nothing, and the trade you wanted is gone.

That trade-off is the whole decision. A limit order gets you a better price or no trade. A market order gets you the trade at whatever the book charges, including the spread and any slippage.

Makers, fees and rewards

On Polymarket, makers pay no fee. Only takers do. Makers can also earn maker rebates, and resting orders close to the midpoint can qualify for liquidity rewards. More in what liquidity is on Polymarket.
