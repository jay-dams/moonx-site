Prediction markets vs sports betting: what is actually different

Buying Yes on a team to win looks a lot like placing a bet. The experience is close, the plumbing is not. Here is what changes for you, point by point, without the marketing on either side.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

With a bookmaker, the bookmaker sets the odds, builds in a margin and takes the other side of your bet. On a prediction market like Polymarket, you trade with other people at a price that moves, the platform earns a trading fee instead of a margin, and you can sell your position before the end. How the law treats each one depends on the country.

Odds versus prices

A bookmaker quotes odds like 2.50. A prediction market quotes a price like 40¢. They express the same thing: 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.50. A 40¢ share that wins pays $1, which is 2.5 times your money. The table in prediction market odds converts any price into decimal and American odds.

Where the margin goes

Add up the implied probabilities of all outcomes in a bookmaker's market and you usually get more than 100%. The excess, often called the overround, is the bookmaker's margin. It's built into every price you see.

On a prediction market, Yes plus No add up to about $1, with a small spread between buying and selling prices. The platform's income is a separate trading fee. On Polymarket that fee depends on the category and the price, and it's zero on some markets (see Polymarket fees). Neither model is free. The difference is that a fee is visible, while a margin is hidden inside the odds.

Selling before the end

This is the biggest practical difference. On a prediction market your position is an asset you can sell to another trader at the current price, whenever you want. If your team goes 2-0 up at half-time, the price of your Yes shares jumps, and you can take the gain then.

Many bookmakers offer a cash-out too, but the bookmaker sets that price, and it can be withdrawn at any time. On a market, the price comes from other traders.

Who you're up against

A bookmaker profits when bettors lose, so it manages risk: it adjusts odds, and some limit customers who win too often. On a prediction market, the platform earns the same fee whoever wins. You are trading against other people, some of them very well informed. That cuts both ways: no one limits you for winning, but the price you're buying is often a sharp one.

What about the law?

Rules differ a lot from one country to another. Some regulators treat prediction markets as financial contracts, others treat them as gambling, and some block them. Polymarket restricts access in a number of countries. Check what applies where you live before you trade.

Which one suits you?

  • If you want fixed odds on a single game and you're happy with a bookmaker's margin, a bookmaker does the job.
  • If you want to trade the price as things happen, sell early, or bet on things beyond sports, a prediction market fits better.

Common questions

Is Polymarket a betting site?

Polymarket is a prediction market: traders buy and sell shares in outcomes with each other, and Polymarket doesn't set odds or take the other side. How a country classifies it legally is a separate question with different answers in different places.

Are prediction market odds better than bookmaker odds?

Often they are close to fair, because there is no margin built into the price. But you also pay a trading fee and a spread, so compare the full cost on the specific market.

Can I bet on sports on Polymarket?

Yes. Polymarket lists many sports markets, from single matches to league titles. On most of them, takers pay a fee at a rate of 0.05 under Polymarket's fee formula.
