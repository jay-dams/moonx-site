What is expected value in prediction markets?

Expected value is the one number that says whether a trade is worth taking, if your estimate of the chance is right. Fees make it smaller than most people think.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Expected value, or EV, is what a trade would return on average if you could repeat it many times. For a share that pays $1, EV per share is your estimated chance times $1, minus the price, minus fees. Buying at 40¢ when you think the chance is 50% gives +10¢ per share before fees, and about +8.4¢ after fees on a sports market through MOONX.

The formula

Call p the price you pay for a share (in dollars) and q your own estimate of the chance it wins.

  • EV per share = q × $1 − p − fees per share
  • EV per dollar, before fees = q ÷ p − 1

If EV is positive, the trade pays on average. If it's zero or negative, it doesn't. When q equals p, EV before fees is zero: you agree with the market, so you have no edge. For a No share, use (1 − q) and the No price.

Fees per share on Polymarket: rate × p × (1 − p). On MOONX, add 1% of p.

A worked example

Worked example

Yes trades at 40¢ on a sports market (fee rate 0.05). You think the real chance is 50%. You put in $100: 100 ÷ 0.40 = 250 shares.

  • EV before fees: 0.50 × $1 − $0.40 = +10¢ per share, so 250 × $0.10 = +$25
  • Polymarket fee: 250 × 0.05 × 0.40 × 0.60 = $3.00
  • MOONX fee: 1% of $100 = $1.00
  • EV after fees: $25 − $3 − $1 = +$21, or about 8.4¢ per share

Check: if Yes wins you get $250, a profit of $250 − $104 = $146. If it loses you lose $104. Half of each: 0.5 × $146 − 0.5 × $104 = $21.

Fees can wipe out a small edge

Worked example: a thin edge

Yes trades at 60¢ on a crypto market (fee rate 0.07). You think the chance is 62%.

  • EV before fees: 0.62 − 0.60 = +2¢ per share
  • Polymarket fee: 0.07 × 0.60 × 0.40 = 1.68¢ per share
  • MOONX fee: 1% of 60¢ = 0.6¢ per share
  • EV after fees: 2 − 1.68 − 0.6 = −0.28¢ per share

On 100 shares that's about −$0.28. A trade that looked good on paper loses money on average.

The spread works the same way. You buy at the ask, so use the ask as p, not the price shown on screen. Selling before the end adds a second round of fees. Holding to resolution has no fee at the end. More in Polymarket fees.

What EV doesn't tell you

  • One trade is not an average. In the first example you either make $146 or lose $104. The +$21 only shows up across many similar, independent trades.
  • EV is only as good as q. The price is the market's estimate, built from many traders with money at stake. If your 50% is really 40%, your positive EV is an illusion.
  • Long shots are the classic trap. A 2026 working paper on 588 million Polymarket trades found that, in aggregate, purchases below 10¢ lost 19.3¢ per dollar. See long shots on Polymarket.
