Multi-outcome markets on Polymarket: elections, awards and "Other"

"Who will win the election?" has more than two answers. Polymarket handles it by giving each candidate its own Yes and No market, linked so that only one can win. Here is how to read them, and the one outcome to avoid.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

A multi-outcome market on Polymarket is a group of Yes/No markets, one per possible answer, where only one can resolve Yes. The Yes prices across all answers add up to about $1. Buying No on one candidate is economically the same as betting on all the others, which Polymarket calls negative risk. If new names can be added later, avoid trading the placeholder "Other".

One question, many small markets

Take "Who will win the Ballon d'Or?". Polymarket lists each candidate with its own price, say:

CandidateYes priceMultiple if right
Player A52¢1.9x
Player B27¢3.7x
Player C12¢8.3x
Player D9¢11.1x

These are illustrative numbers. Behind the scenes, each line is a separate Yes/No market: "Will Player A win?", "Will Player B win?", and so on. Exactly one of them will resolve Yes.

Why the prices add up to about $1

Since only one candidate wins, holding one Yes share of every candidate guarantees exactly $1 at the end. So the Yes prices across the whole list should add up to about $1, give or take the spread. If they add up to much more or less, traders step in and the gap closes.

This is also a quick sanity check when you read a market: if the favourite is at 52¢, the others share about 48¢ between them.

Buying No on one candidate

You can buy No on any line. "No on Player A" pays $1 if anyone other than Player A wins. Polymarket links these markets with a mechanism it calls negative risk: one No share on a candidate can be converted into one Yes share on every other candidate in the event, in a single transaction.

What it means for you: betting against one outcome is economically the same as betting on all the others, without having to buy each of them.

Worked example

You think Player A is overrated at 52¢. You buy No on Player A at about 48¢. If Player B, C or anyone else wins, each share pays $1: about 2.1x. If Player A wins, you lose your stake.

The "Other" trap

Some events start before every candidate is known, like a party's nominee a year before the primaries. Polymarket can add new names later, using placeholders. If the winner is never named in the list, the market resolves to "Other", and what "Other" covers can change as placeholders are clarified.

Polymarket's own documentation advises trading only named outcomes and avoiding "Other" directly. Follow that advice.

Tips for reading multi-outcome markets

  • Check the volume per line, not just the event. A candidate at 3¢ may have very few buyers and sellers.
  • Read the rules once for the whole event. They define what counts as winning for every line.
  • Watch for withdrawals. If a candidate drops out, their line usually goes to almost zero and the others reprice within minutes.

Common questions

How do multiple-choice markets work on Polymarket?

Each possible answer is its own Yes/No market, and only one answer can resolve Yes. Yes on the winning answer pays $1, and Yes on every other answer pays nothing. No on any losing answer pays $1.

What is negative risk on Polymarket?

A link between the markets of one event: a No share on one outcome can be converted into a Yes share on each of the other outcomes. It makes betting against one outcome the same as betting on all the others.

Should I buy "Other"?

Polymarket's documentation advises against trading "Other" directly in events where new outcomes can be added, because what it covers can change.
