How Polymarket works

Polymarket fees explained, with real numbers

Polymarket charges a fee on most trades, and it is not a flat percentage. It depends on the category and on the price you trade at. Here is the formula, a table in plain percentages, and a full example from buy to sell.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·4 min read
Short answer

On most Polymarket markets, the trader whose order fills right away (the taker) pays a fee of shares × rate × price × (1 − price). Rates go from 0 for geopolitics to 0.07 for crypto. As a share of the money you spend, that's the rate times (1 − price): about 3.5% at 50¢ on a crypto market, 2.5% on sports, 2% on politics. Makers pay nothing, and there is no fee when a market resolves.

Who pays, and when

Polymarket uses an order book. Some orders wait in the book (they make liquidity). Others fill against them right away (they take liquidity). According to Polymarket's documentation, takers pay a fee on most markets and makers pay none. The fee is applied when the trade matches, in dollars.

There is no fee when a market resolves and your winning shares pay $1. Polymarket also charges nothing to deposit or withdraw, though the service you use to move money may charge its own fee.

The formula

For a trade of C shares at a price p:

fee = C × rate × p × (1 − p)

Because of the p × (1 − p) part, the fee in dollars is highest at 50¢ and shrinks toward the extremes. There is a simpler way to read it: divided by what you spend (C × p), the fee is just rate × (1 − p) of your stake.

The pattern matters: cheap shares carry the highest fee in percentage terms. A long shot on a crypto market costs almost 7% of your stake to enter. A near-certain favourite costs a fraction of a percent. When you sell, the same rule applies to what you receive.

A full example, from buy to sell

Worked example

You put $20 on Yes at 50¢ in a football market. You get 40 shares.

  • Polymarket fee: 40 × 0.05 × 0.50 × 0.50 = $0.50
  • MOONX fee, 1% of $20: $0.20

Total fees to get in: about $0.70.

If you hold and Yes wins: you receive $40, with no fee at resolution. Fees included, your profit is about $19.30.

If you sell at 70¢ before the end: 40 shares bring in $28. Polymarket's fee is 40 × 0.05 × 0.70 × 0.30 = $0.42 and MOONX's is $0.28, so you receive about $27.30. Your profit is about $6.60, locked in whatever happens next. Numbers are rounded, and the app shows the exact amounts before you confirm.

Ways the fee gets smaller

  • Higher prices cost less. As a share of your stake, the fee is rate × (1 − price), so it shrinks as the price rises toward $1. Cheap long shots carry the biggest fee.
  • Geopolitics is free. Those markets carry no taker fee.
  • Make instead of take. On Polymarket directly, a limit order that waits in the book pays no fee, and makers can earn rebates. The trade-off is that it may never fill.
  • Hold to the end. If you're confident, holding to resolution avoids the fee on the way out.

Polymarket also runs a Taker Rebate Program that returns part of the fees to active traders, in tiers. See its documentation for the current levels.

Common questions

Does Polymarket charge a fee to withdraw?

No. Polymarket charges nothing to deposit or withdraw. The exchange, card provider or bridge you use may charge its own fee.

Do I pay a fee if I win?

No. Fees are charged when a trade matches, on the way in and on the way out if you sell. When a market resolves, winning shares pay $1 each with no fee.

Why is the fee bigger on cheap shares?

In dollars it isn't: it peaks at 50¢. But as a percentage of what you spend it's the rate times (1 − price), so a 5¢ share pays close to the full rate.

Which Polymarket markets have no fee?

Geopolitics and world events markets have a taker rate of zero, according to Polymarket's documentation.
