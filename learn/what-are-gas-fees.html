What are gas fees?

Anything that happens on a blockchain, a payment, a trade, a withdrawal, costs a small network fee. It's called gas, and it's one of the first things that confuses new users.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Gas is the unit that measures how much computing work a blockchain transaction takes, and the gas fee is what you pay for it, the gas used multiplied by the price per unit of gas, paid in the network's own token. On Polygon, where Polymarket runs, that token is POL. You pay for the work done even if a transaction fails. On MOONX you never pay gas, because MOONX pays the network fees on all MOONX transactions.

The definition

A blockchain is run by many computers that check and record every transaction. That work isn't free. Gas is the unit that measures how much work a transaction needs, the way litres measure fuel. The gas fee is what you pay for it:

gas fee = gas used × price per unit of gas

The fee is paid in the network's own token: ETH on Ethereum, POL on Polygon. Ethereum's documentation gives two reasons the fee exists: it pays for the work, and it stops people from spamming the network or running code that loops forever.

A few details that surprise people:

  • The price moves. When the network is busy, each unit of gas costs more.
  • A failed transaction still costs gas for the work done before it failed.
  • Prices are quoted in tiny units. On Ethereum, gas prices are usually given in gwei, one billionth of an ETH.
Worked example

These numbers are made up, just to show the arithmetic.

A transaction uses 50,000 units of gas at a price of 30 gwei per unit.

  • Fee: 50,000 × 30 = 1,500,000 gwei.
  • One gwei is 0.000000001 ETH, so the fee is 1,500,000 × 0.000000001 = 0.0015 ETH.

You would need to hold that ETH in your wallet before sending anything. That is the part that trips up beginners: holding dollars is not enough, you also need the network's token.

Gas on Polymarket

Polymarket runs on Polygon (what chain is Polymarket on?). Its documentation says most of its wallets act through a relayer that submits transactions without gas for the user, while a plain wallet acting on its own needs POL to pay gas. Gas is separate from Polymarket's trading fee, which is charged on most trades when they match (Polymarket fees).

Gas on MOONX

MOONX pays the network fees on all MOONX transactions: adding money to trading, buying, selling, collecting winnings and withdrawing. You never need to buy or hold POL. Your wallet only holds dollars, as USDC on Polygon.
