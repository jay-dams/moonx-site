What is volume on Polymarket?

A market with millions in volume sounds like a market with millions at stake. It isn't, quite. Volume counts trades, and the same shares can trade many times.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Volume on Polymarket is the amount traded on a market over a period, such as the past 24 hours or since the market opened. It measures activity, so the same shares bought and sold ten times count ten times. It is different from open interest, the value held in positions that are still open, and from liquidity, the orders waiting in the book. High volume usually means an active market, but it says nothing about which side is right.

The short answer

Volume is a running total of trades. Every time shares change hands, the trade adds to the market's volume. Polymarket's market data includes, for example, a figure for "trading volume over the past 24 hours."

Volume tells you a market is busy. Busy markets tend to have more orders in the book, which usually means a tighter spread and less slippage when you trade.

Volume is not money at stake

The same shares can be traded again and again, and every trade adds to volume.

Worked example

100 Yes shares change hands 10 times in a day, each time at about 50¢.

Volume in dollars: 10 × 100 × $0.50 = $500. Volume in shares: 10 × 100 = 1,000 shares.

But they are still the same 100 shares. Whoever holds them at the end gets $1 each if Yes wins, so $100. The volume figure is five times that.

The number closer to "money at stake" is open interest. Polymarket's documentation describes it as the value currently held in outstanding positions, in USDC.

Three numbers people mix up

MeasureWhat it counts
VolumeTrades that already happened, over a period
Open interestValue held in positions that are still open
LiquidityOrders waiting in the book right now

A market can have huge volume and thin liquidity: lots of trading earlier, few orders now. It can also have deep liquidity and little volume: plenty of orders waiting, but few people trading. More on the third in what liquidity is on Polymarket.

Why numbers don't always match

Polymarket's data documentation counts volume in different ways for different purposes. Some figures are in shares, some count both sides of each trade, and some count only the side that took the order. Two places can therefore show different totals for similar activity. Treat volume as a sign of how active a market is, not as an exact amount of money.

Apps that send orders to Polymarket through its Builders Program, such as MOONX, can also have their volume credited to them on builders.polymarket.com. See what is a Polymarket builder?.

What volume doesn't tell you

  • Which side is right. Volume is the same whether buyers or sellers were correct.
  • That the price is accurate. Busy markets often price well, but not always. See are prediction markets accurate.
  • Who is trading. One large trader can create a lot of volume.
