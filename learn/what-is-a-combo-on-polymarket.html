What is a combo on Polymarket?

A combo is Polymarket's version of a parlay. Several outcomes, one position, one price. It pays more because every part has to come true.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

A combo on Polymarket combines two or more outcomes, called legs, into a single pair of Yes and No positions. The combo's Yes pays only if every leg wins, and its No pays if any leg loses. The price doesn't come from the order book. Market makers quote it when you ask, and you accept or let the quote expire. Polymarket's fee rate for combos is 0.05, whatever the categories of the legs.

The short answer

A combo takes outcomes from different markets and joins them into one position. Each outcome is a leg. Polymarket's documentation describes a combo's Yes as paying out "when every leg in the combination pays out," and its No as the complement: it pays when the full combination does not.

  • A combo has from 2 to 50 legs.
  • Legs must come from markets that are enabled for combos. Not every market is.
  • Contradictory legs can't be combined. You can't pick both Yes and No on the same outcome.

How the price is set

A normal Polymarket trade fills against the order book. A combo works by request for quote:

  1. You choose your legs and an amount, and send a request.
  2. Connected market makers have 400 milliseconds to send a price.
  3. You get the best quote and have a few seconds to accept it.
  4. If you accept, the trade usually executes (the market maker can have a one-second last look). If no maker quotes, there is no trade.

Because makers set the price, a combo can cost more or less than multiplying the legs' prices together. Treat that product as a rough guide, not the price you will get.

Worked example

Leg 1: Yes at 60¢. Leg 2: Yes at 50¢. If the two are unrelated, both happening has a chance of about 0.60 × 0.50 = 30%.

Say a maker quotes 30¢. For $15 you get 15 ÷ 0.30 = 50 shares. At the combo rate of 0.05, the fee is 50 × 0.05 × 0.30 × 0.70 = about $0.53.

If both legs win, you receive $50, a profit of about 50 − 15 − 0.53 = $34.47. If either leg loses, you get $0. At a 30% chance, that happens about 7 times in 10.

Real quotes depend on the makers, and legs that are linked to each other, where one outcome makes the other more likely, won't follow the simple product.

Fees and selling

Polymarket's fee table lists a 0.05 rate for combos, and the documentation says every combo uses it, whatever the categories of its legs. When you buy, the quote includes fees in the total. Selling a combo goes through the same quote process, sized in shares, with the amount you receive shown after fees.

When some legs have resolved and others haven't, Polymarket can simplify the position so it keeps only the legs still open.

What to keep in mind

  • Every extra leg makes the combo less likely to win. The bigger payout is the price of that.
  • A combo with many legs is a long shot, with the same risk: most of them lose.
  • Check each leg's rules, as on any market. One leg resolving against you is enough to lose.

For events where only one outcome can win, Polymarket has a different structure, explained in multi-outcome markets.
