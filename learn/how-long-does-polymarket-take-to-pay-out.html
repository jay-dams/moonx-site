How long does Polymarket take to pay out?

A Polymarket market doesn't pay the second an event ends. The result goes through a short challenge window first, and a longer process if someone disagrees.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

After an event, someone proposes the result to Polymarket's oracle and a 2-hour challenge window opens. If nobody disputes it, the market resolves and each winning share is worth $1. A disputed result takes about 4 to 6 days in total. On MOONX, the money then lands in your wallet usually within minutes, sometimes a few hours.

The timeline

Most Polymarket markets are resolved through the UMA Optimistic Oracle. The clock starts when someone proposes the result and posts a bond, typically $750.

StageHow long
Event ends, result proposedNot fixed, depends on the market
Challenge window2 hours
Undisputed total, from the proposalAbout 2 hours
Second dispute: debate, then UMA vote24 to 48 hours, then about 48 hours
Disputed totalAbout 4 to 6 days

The first dispute throws out the proposal and starts a new round. Only a second dispute goes to a vote of UMA token holders. More on UMA.

Short crypto Up or Down markets skip all of this. They settle automatically from a Chainlink price feed, with no proposal and no dispute. See Up or Down markets.

Worked example

A match ends at 10 pm. Say someone proposes the result at 10:30 pm. Nobody disputes it, so it's final at about 12:30 am. On MOONX, the money is usually in your wallet within minutes after that.

If the result were disputed twice, the same market could take about 4 to 6 days to pay.

Selling instead of waiting

You don't have to wait for the payout. Once the result is obvious, the price usually climbs close to $1, and you can sell your shares to another trader. It costs you a little.

Worked example: selling at 99¢

You hold 100 shares of the winning side on a sports market. The best bid is 99¢.

  • Sale: 100 × 0.99 = $99
  • Polymarket fee: 100 × 0.05 × 0.99 × 0.01 = about $0.05
  • MOONX fee: 1% of $99 = $0.99

You keep about $97.96 now, against $100 if you wait for resolution, where there is no fee.

Whether $2.04 is worth paying for speed is your call. During a dispute, prices can swing again, so selling early also removes that risk. More in selling before the result.
