What are Polymarket Up or Down markets?

The fastest markets on Polymarket ask one thing. Will a crypto price be higher or lower at the end of a short window? Here is how they start, end and settle.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Polymarket Up or Down markets ask whether a crypto pair, such as BTC/USD, ends a set time window higher or lower than it started. Both the starting price and the final price come from the same Chainlink time-weighted average price, and the market resolves Up if the final price is equal to or greater than the start, Down if it is lower. They settle without UMA's proposal and dispute process, and they carry the crypto taker fee rate of 0.07.

How one works

Each Up or Down market names an asset pair, a start time and a duration. Polymarket's documentation gives the example of whether BTC/USD moves Up or Down during a one-hour window. Its liquidity rewards page names other crypto markets, of 5 minutes, 15 minutes and 4 hours, that also settle on a Chainlink TWAP.

  1. At the start, a price is recorded. Polymarket calls it the price to beat.
  2. At the end of the window, the final price is recorded.
  3. If the final price is equal to or greater than the price to beat, Up wins. If it is lower, Down wins.

Each winning share pays $1, each losing share $0, as on any Polymarket market.

Where the prices come from

Both prices come from the same Chainlink stream, a TWAP: a time-weighted average price over a defined lookback window. So the final price is an average over a short period, not the price at one exact second.

Because the result follows directly from these observations, these markets resolve without UMA's proposal and dispute process. The same data always gives the same result. Other markets work differently, see what UMA is on Polymarket.

The tie rule

A tie goes to Up. If the final price is exactly the price to beat, Up wins. It rarely matters, but read it before you buy Down.

Fees, and why they add up

Up or Down markets are crypto markets, and Polymarket's crypto taker rate is 0.07, the highest of its categories. At 50¢, that's 0.07 × (1 − 0.50) = 3.5% of the money you spend.

Worked example

You buy $10 of Up at 50¢: 10 ÷ 0.50 = 20 shares.

Polymarket fee: 20 × 0.07 × 0.50 × 0.50 = $0.35.

If Up wins, you receive $20, a profit of 20 − 10 − 0.35 = $9.65. On MOONX, add its 1% fee, $0.10, for a profit of $9.55.

Now repeat it 10 times, $10 each at 50¢, and win exactly half. You get back 5 × $20 = $100 on $100 staked, and you've paid 10 × $0.35 = $3.50 in Polymarket fees. Breaking even on wins still loses money.

What to keep in mind

  • Price moves over a few minutes are hard to predict. A 50¢ price says the market sees the outcome as close to even.
  • Fees are charged on every trade, so many short trades add up fast, as the example shows.
  • At a price near 50¢, a win roughly doubles your money, minus fees. Bigger multiples only come from buying a side the market thinks is unlikely.
