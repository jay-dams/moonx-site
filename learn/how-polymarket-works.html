How does Polymarket work? Shares, prices and payouts

Polymarket is one of the largest prediction markets in the world. Under the hood it is an order book where people trade Yes and No shares that pay $1 if they are right. Here is what happens when you click Buy.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·5 min read
Short answer

On Polymarket, every question has Yes and No shares priced between 0 and $1. You buy from other traders through an order book, you can sell any time before the end, and when the market resolves each winning share pays $1. The price shown is usually the middle of the best buy and sell offers, so the price you actually pay can be a little higher.

The short version

  1. Each market asks a question with a deadline and written rules.
  2. You buy Yes or No shares. Each share pays $1 if it's right, $0 if it's wrong.
  3. Prices move between 0 and $1 as people trade. The price reads as a probability: 25¢ means about 25%.
  4. You can sell your shares to other traders at any time before the market ends.
  5. When the answer is known, the market resolves and winning shares pay out.

The rest of this guide explains each step and the details that trip people up.

Yes and No are two sides of one dollar

On Polymarket, a Yes share and a No share together are always worth exactly $1, because one of them will pay. That's why their prices mirror each other. Polymarket's own documentation gives the example of a Yes buyer at 60¢ being matched with a No buyer at 40¢: together they put up $1, and each receives their share.

In practice this means "buy No at 40¢" and "sell Yes at 60¢" are almost the same trade. You don't need to understand it to trade, but it explains why Yes and No prices always add up to roughly $1.

The price you see, and the price you pay

Polymarket runs an order book. Buyers post the highest price they are willing to pay (the bid). Sellers post the lowest price they will accept (the ask). The gap between them is the spread.

According to Polymarket's documentation, the price displayed on a market is the midpoint of the best bid and best ask. If the spread is wider than 10¢, it shows the last traded price instead.

Worked example

The best bid for Yes is 34¢ and the best ask is 40¢. The market shows 37¢, the midpoint.

If you buy right now, you pay the ask: 40¢. If you sell right now, you get the bid: 34¢. Buy and immediately sell, and you lose 6¢ a share before any fee.

On busy markets the spread is often a single tick, so the difference is small. On quiet markets it can be wide. Always look at the price on the buy button, not just the headline number.

Market orders and limit orders

There are two ways to trade:

  • A market order fills right away against the best offers in the book. You get speed, but if your order is large compared with what's available, part of it can fill at worse prices.
  • A limit order sets the worst price you will accept. It can sit in the book until someone takes it, or until you cancel it.

MOONX keeps this simple: you choose Yes or No and an amount, you see the price and the cost, and you confirm.

Minimums and sizes

Each Polymarket market sets its own minimum order size, counted in shares, and its own tick size (the smallest price step, often 1¢, sometimes smaller). The minimum is counted in shares, so on many markets a trade can start at around a dollar.

What you hold, and where

Your shares are tokens on the Polygon blockchain. Polymarket settles trades in pUSD, a dollar token backed by USDC that it introduced in 2026 to replace the bridged USDC it used before. When you sell, or when you win, you receive dollars back.

Because everything is on a public blockchain, every trade is visible. That's why you can look up any trader's positions, and why apps can show you what other people are trading.

How a market ends

When the event is over, someone proposes the result and posts a bond. If nobody disputes it within 2 hours, the result stands and winning shares can be redeemed for $1 each. Disputes go through a longer process, typically 4 to 6 days, run by the UMA Optimistic Oracle. Short crypto "Up or Down" markets settle automatically from a Chainlink price feed instead. The details are in how Polymarket markets are resolved.

What it costs

On most markets, the trader who takes an existing offer pays a taker fee that depends on the category and on the price. Some categories, such as geopolitics, have no fee. Deposits and withdrawals are free on Polymarket's side. The full table, with examples in cents, is in Polymarket fees, explained.

Common questions

Is Polymarket a bookmaker?

No. Polymarket doesn't set odds or take the other side of your trade. Prices come from traders buying and selling with each other in an order book.

Can I sell before the market ends?

Yes, at any time, as long as someone is willing to buy. On busy markets that is instant. On quiet ones you may have to accept a lower price. See selling before the result.

What happens to my money if I'm right?

Each winning share pays $1 when the market resolves. If you bought 50 Yes shares at 20¢ for $10 and Yes wins, you receive $50.

Is Polymarket available everywhere?

No. Polymarket blocks or restricts access in a number of countries, and the list changes. MOONX follows Polymarket's restrictions and adds a few of its own, listed in its Terms. Check the rules where you live before you trade.
