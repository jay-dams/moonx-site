Selling before the result: take profit or cut a loss on Polymarket

A prediction market is not a ticket you hold until the final whistle. You can sell your shares to another trader whenever you want. Beginners often forget it.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·4 min read
Short answer

Yes. Polymarket shares can be sold at any time before the market resolves, to anyone who wants to buy them. You get the current bid price, minus fees. If you bought at 10¢ and the price is now 40¢, you can sell for about 4x your money today, without waiting to see who is right. On quiet markets a sale can take longer or need a lower price.

You can be wrong and still make money

This is the part that surprises people. Say you buy Yes at 10¢ on a team to win the league. Halfway through the season they are top of the table and Yes trades at 40¢. You sell. You have made 4x, before fees. If the team collapses in the last month, you don't care: you already sold.

The reverse works too. If the news turns against you, selling at 4¢ what you bought at 10¢ gets you 40% of your money back. Waiting might get you zero.

What price you get

When you sell straight away, your shares fill against the best bids in the order book, the highest prices other traders are offering. That's a little below the price shown on the market, which is usually the midpoint between bids and asks.

  • On busy markets the gap is often a single tick, and a sale is instant.
  • On quiet markets the best bid can be several cents lower, and there may not be enough buyers for all your shares at one price. You can wait, or accept a lower price.

Fees apply on the way out too: Polymarket's taker fee where the category has one, and on MOONX, 1% of the amount. See Polymarket fees.

Worked example

You bought 100 shares of Yes at 12¢: $12. The market now shows 47¢, with the best bid at 46¢.

Selling all 100 at 46¢ brings in $46. On a sports market, Polymarket's fee is 100 × 0.05 × 0.46 × 0.54 = about $1.24, and MOONX's is $0.46. You keep about $44.30. Counting the fees you paid to buy, about $0.65, that's roughly 3.5x your money, locked in.

When selling early makes sense

  • The price has moved a lot in your favour. Going from 10¢ to 50¢ is 5x. Going from 50¢ to $1 is only 2x more, and that last part carries all the risk of being wrong. Many traders take part or all of the gain here.
  • Your reason for the trade is gone. You bought because a player was fit, and now he's injured. The original idea no longer holds, so the position has no reason to stay.
  • You need the money for something else. Money in a market that ends in six months is money you can't use.
  • The market is about to become volatile. Before a debate, a match or a court ruling, prices can jump either way in minutes.

When holding makes sense

  • You still think the real chance is higher than the price. That's the only good reason to hold. At 90¢, holding to $1 is worth it only if you're more than 90% sure.
  • The share is almost at $1. Near the end, selling at 97¢ removes only 3¢ of risk per share and still costs a sell fee.

Selling part of a position

You don't have to choose all or nothing. A common approach: sell enough to get back what you put in, and let the rest ride for free. If you bought 100 shares at 20¢ ($20) and the price is 50¢, selling 40 shares returns about $20. The other 60 shares cost you nothing now.

Common questions

Can you cash out early on Polymarket?

Yes. You can sell your shares to other traders at any time before the market resolves, at the current bid price.

Why did I get less than the price shown?

The price on the market is usually the midpoint between the best bid and the best ask. Selling right away fills at the bid, which is lower, and fees are taken from the amount.

What if nobody wants to buy my shares?

On very quiet markets there may be few buyers. You can post a sale at a higher price and wait, or lower your price. On busy markets this is rarely a problem.

Is it better to sell or hold until the end?

Hold if you still believe the real chance is higher than the current price. Sell if your reason for the trade has changed, if the gain is large, or if you need the money.
