Is Polymarket safe?

There is no yes or no answer. Polymarket removes some risks you'd find elsewhere and adds others.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Polymarket's contracts run on the Polygon blockchain and have been audited by several firms, including Cantina, Quantstamp and Certora, which lowers but doesn't remove the risk of a bug. The main risks are elsewhere. A result can be disputed and take 4 to 6 days, a thin market can make selling expensive, a wallet you control can't be recovered if you lose access, and scams pose as apps or support. You can also lose your whole stake on any trade.

What Polymarket does and doesn't do

It helps to know what Polymarket is not:

  • It doesn't set the odds. Its help center says prices come from supply and demand between users.
  • It isn't the other side of your bet. The counterparty to each trade is another user, not a house.
  • It doesn't decide results. Most markets are settled through the UMA oracle (what is UMA?).
  • It doesn't guarantee a buyer. If nobody wants your shares at a fair price, you wait or accept a worse one.

The risks, one by one

1. Smart contracts. Your money and shares sit in contracts on Polygon. Polymarket lists audits from Cantina, Quantstamp, Certora, SigmaPrime, Zellic and Pashov in 2026, and a bug bounty through Cantina. Audits reduce the chance of a flaw. They don't make it zero.

2. Resolution disputes. A result follows the market's written rules, not what most people expected. A disputed market goes to a vote of UMA token holders and takes about 4 to 6 days. A market that can't be answered under its rules can pay $0.50 per share to both sides (what happens if a market is unclear?).

3. Thin markets. With few orders in the book, the gap between buy and sell prices is wide. When that gap passes 10¢, Polymarket shows the last traded price, which may not be a price you can get (what is the spread?).

4. Losing access to your own wallet. If you control your wallet, nobody can restore it for you. On MOONX, losing the backup file or its password means the money is lost for good, by design (what if I lose my backup file?).

5. Sending to the wrong place. Polymarket's help center warns that transfers to the wrong chain or address cannot be reversed.

6. Scams posing as apps. Fake websites, fake apps and fake "support" accounts can copy a real product's name. No real support team needs your backup file, password or PIN. Type the address yourself and check it.

Worked example

You buy $40 of Yes at 80¢: 40 ÷ 0.80 = 50 shares (before fees).

  • Yes wins: 50 × $1 = $50.
  • No wins: $0. The $40 is gone.
  • The market is unclear and resolves 50-50: 50 × $0.50 = $25.
  • You sell early in a thin market where the best bid is 70¢: 50 × 0.70 = $35, before fees.

None of these outcomes takes more than the $40 you put in, plus fees (can you lose more than you bet?).
