What is slippage on Polymarket?

The best price in the book only applies to the shares offered at that price. Buy more than that and the rest fills higher. That gap is slippage.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Slippage on Polymarket is the difference between the best price in the order book and the average price your order actually gets. A market order fills against the cheapest offers first, and if there aren't enough shares at that price, it moves on to the next, more expensive ones. The larger your order compared with what's in the book, the worse your average price. Small orders on busy markets see little or none.

The short answer

An order book is a queue of offers at different prices. A market order takes the best offer first. If that offer is smaller than your order, it takes the next one, then the next. Traders call this walking the book.

Each step up costs a little more, so your average price ends up above the best price you saw. That difference is slippage. Polymarket's documentation warns that "large orders may move the price significantly" and advises: "Always check orderbook depth before trading in size."

Worked example

You send a market order to buy $148 of Yes. The best ask is 40¢, so you might expect 148 ÷ 0.40 = 370 shares.

Here is how it actually fills, before fees:

  • 100 shares at 40¢ = $40. $108 left.
  • 150 shares at 42¢ = $63. $45 left.
  • 100 shares at 45¢ = $45. Done.

You get 100 + 150 + 100 = 350 shares. Your average price is 148 ÷ 350 = about 42.3¢, not 40¢.

That's 20 fewer shares. If Yes wins, you collect $350 instead of $370. Your order also cleared the two cheapest levels, so the next buyer now faces a best ask of 45¢.

Selling works the same way in reverse: a large sale fills against the highest bids first, then lower ones.

What limits the damage

On Polymarket, a market order for a buy is set in dollars and a sell in shares. Market orders never wait in the book: any part that can't fill is cancelled. Some order types fill everything or nothing (FOK), others fill what they can and cancel the rest (FAK). Polymarket's tools also let an order set a worst acceptable price, so a buy can't cross above it.

In practice:

  • Size to the book. If your order is small compared with the shares offered at the best price, slippage is close to zero.
  • Prefer busy markets. More resting orders means more shares at each price. See what liquidity is on Polymarket.
  • Use a limit order on Polymarket directly when price matters more than speed. See limit orders.
  • Split a large sale into smaller ones over time on a thin market.
