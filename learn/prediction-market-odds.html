Prediction market odds: how a price becomes a payout

On a prediction market, the price is the odds. A share at 20¢ means about a 20% chance and pays 5 times your money if it lands. Here is the math, a table you can keep, and the details that shave a little off.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·4 min read
Short answer

Divide $1 by the price to get your payout multiple. A share at 20¢ pays $1, so it returns 5x your money if you're right, and the market gives it about a 20% chance. At 50¢ it's 2x, at 5¢ it's 20x, at 90¢ it's about 1.11x. Fees and the spread lower the real multiple a little.

Three numbers from one price

Every share on Polymarket pays $1 if it's right. So the price alone tells you three things:

  • Implied probability: the price in cents is the chance in percent. 20¢ is about 20%.
  • Payout multiple: $1 divided by the price. 20¢ gives 1 ÷ 0.20 = 5x.
  • What $10 becomes: $10 buys 10 ÷ 0.20 = 50 shares, which pay $50 if you're right.

The multiple is also what sports bettors call decimal odds. A 5x share is the same as odds of 5.00.

The formulas

If the price is p (in dollars, so 20¢ is 0.20):

  • Multiple, or decimal odds: 1 ÷ p
  • Shares you get for an amount: amount ÷ p
  • Profit if you're right: amount × (1 ÷ p − 1)
  • American odds: if p is 0.50 or under, +100 × (1 − p) ÷ p. If p is over 0.50, −100 × p ÷ (1 − p).

When is a trade worth taking?

A price is the market's estimate. You only have an edge if you think the real chance is higher than the price.

Worked example

A share trades at 20¢. You believe the real chance is 30%.

For every $1 you put in, you get 5 shares. On average you expect 30% × $5 = $1.50 back. That's a positive expected value of 50¢ per dollar, before fees.

If you think the real chance is 15%, the same trade expects 15% × $5 = $0.75 back per dollar. You would be paying for a long shot that's priced too high.

This is the only test that matters, and it's humbling. The market price already combines what thousands of people know. Beating it on a regular basis is hard.

Why the real multiple is a bit lower

Four things sit between the table and your result:

  1. The spread. You buy at the ask, which can be a cent or more above the price on the screen. At low prices a cent matters a lot: buying at 6¢ instead of 5¢ turns 20x into 16.7x.
  2. Polymarket's taker fee. On most markets, an order that fills right away pays a fee. As a share of the money you spend, it works out to the fee rate times (1 − price). On a crypto market (rate 0.07), buying at 20¢ costs about 5.6% of your stake. At 90¢ it's 0.7%. Geopolitics markets have no fee. Details in Polymarket fees, explained.
  3. The app's fee. MOONX charges 1% of the trade value when you buy and when you sell. There is no fee when a market resolves.
  4. Time. Your money is tied up until the market ends. A 90¢ share that pays $1 in a year is a different deal from one that pays tomorrow.
Worked example: fees included

$10 on Yes at 20¢ on a crypto market: 50 shares. Polymarket's fee is about $0.56 and MOONX's is $0.10, so the trade costs you about $10.66 in all. If Yes wins you receive $50: a multiple of about 4.7x instead of 5x. Numbers are rounded, and the app shows the exact amounts before you confirm.

Selling early changes the math

You don't have to wait for $1. If you bought at 20¢ and the price climbs to 60¢, you can sell and take 3x right away, whatever happens next. On MOONX that's one button, Eject. Many good trades are closed this way. See selling before the result.

Common questions

What do Polymarket odds mean?

The price of a share is the market's estimate of the probability. Yes at 35¢ means traders, as a group, put the chance at about 35%.

How do I calculate my payout on Polymarket?

Divide the amount you put in by the price to get your number of shares. Each share pays $1 if you're right. $25 at 40¢ is 62.5 shares, so $62.50 if you win, before fees.

Are cheap shares a better deal?

Not by default. A 5¢ share pays 20x, but the market thinks it wins about 1 time in 20. Studies of betting and prediction markets often find long shots slightly overpriced. Read long shots on Polymarket before you chase them.

Why do Yes and No not add up to exactly $1?

Because of the spread. The best ask for Yes plus the best ask for No is usually a cent or two above $1. That gap is what you pay to trade instantly.
