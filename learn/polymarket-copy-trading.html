Polymarket copy trading: follow a trade, or take the other side

Every Polymarket trade is public, so you can see what other people buy and follow them. Done well, it's a shortcut to good ideas. Done blindly, it's a way to buy late and sell late. Here is how to do it with your eyes open.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Yes, you can follow Polymarket traders, because every trade is recorded on a public blockchain. But you will rarely get their price: by the time you see a trade, the market has often moved. Check the price you would pay, the size of their position, the market's volume and whether they are still in. Why you can see other people's trades

Polymarket runs on the Polygon blockchain. Every position and every trade is recorded publicly, which is why each Polymarket account has a public profile with its positions and results. It's also why apps can build feeds, leaderboards and alerts around what traders do.

Copying is not getting the same trade

Following a trader sounds simple: they buy, you buy. In practice, three things differ.

  1. Your price. If a well-known trader buys Yes at 30¢, others follow and the price climbs. You might pay 36¢. Their 3.3x becomes your 2.8x.
  2. Your exit. They may sell the next day, quietly. If you don't see it, you're holding a position its original owner has already left.
  3. Their size. A $50,000 position can be a small hedge inside a big book. Your $20 copy of it is a full bet.
Worked example

A trader buys Yes at 25¢. You see it an hour later and buy at 31¢. The market resolves Yes: they make 4x, you make about 3.2x before fees. Now suppose they sold at 45¢ after a news spike and the market then resolved No: they made 1.8x, and you lost your stake.

What to check before you follow a trade

  • The price now, not their price. Would you buy at today's price if nobody had bought before you?
  • Their record over many trades. One huge win says little. A long history across different markets says more.
  • Whether they're still in. Look at their current position, not just the entry.
  • The market's volume. On a thin market, followers can push the price up just by arriving.
  • The rules of the market. You are betting on the written rules, not on the trader's opinion.

Taking the other side

Following works in both directions. If a trader you think is reckless piles into a long shot, the opposite side may be the better trade. Since Yes and No always add up to about $1, taking the other side is a full trade in its own right, with its own price and payout.

Common questions

Can you copy trade on Polymarket?

You can follow other traders because all trades are public. There's no guarantee you'll get the same price, and you need to watch when they sell. Some apps, like MOONX, let you open someone's trade and take the same side or the opposite one in a tap.

Can I see what other Polymarket traders are buying?

Yes. Polymarket profiles show positions and activity, and every trade is recorded on the Polygon blockchain.

Is copying the best traders a good strategy?

It can surface good ideas, but you will usually enter later and at a worse price, and you may miss their exit. Judge the trade at the price you would pay.

Do I pay more when I trade from someone's post on MOONX?

No. The reward for the author comes out of MOONX's fee share. You pay the same fees as any other trade.
