What is the minimum bet on Polymarket?

There is no single dollar minimum on Polymarket. There is a floor in shares, and a floor of about $1 for orders that fill right away. Which one counts depends on the price.

By MOONX Team·Updated 27 October 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Each Polymarket market sets a minimum order size in shares, and 5 shares is common. In practice, Polymarket's order system also rejects an order that fills right away when it's worth under $1. So the smallest instant trade is the larger of 5 × the price and about $1: just over $1 at prices up to 20¢, and 5 × the price above 20¢ ($2.50 at 50¢, $4.50 at 90¢), before fees.

Two floors

The share floor. Polymarket's order book has a setting for each market called the minimum order size: the smallest number of shares it accepts in one order. Polymarket's documentation uses 5 shares as its example, and that was the minimum on every one of the 100 busiest markets we checked on 27 October 2026.

The dollar floor. An order that fills right away against the book, called a marketable order, also has to be worth at least $1. This isn't written in Polymarket's official documentation. It's how the order book behaves in practice: developers who send a smaller instant buy get back the error "invalid amount for a marketable BUY order ($X), min size: $1". MOONX's own site states that trades start at just over $1.

When you buy, you choose an amount in dollars. That amount is turned into shares: dollars ÷ price. The order has to clear both floors.

Worked example

You want to put $2 on a market where Yes costs 50¢. That buys 2 ÷ 0.50 = 4 shares, one short of a 5-share minimum. You'd need at least 5 × 0.50 = $2.50.

Now you want to put $0.50 on a share at 5¢. That buys 0.50 ÷ 0.05 = 10 shares, above the share floor, but the order is worth less than $1, so an instant buy is rejected. You'd need just over $1.

Small trades and fees

Fees don't punish small trades. Polymarket's taker fee is calculated per share: shares × rate × price × (1 − price). There's no fixed charge per order. MOONX's fee is 1% of the trade value, so about 1¢ on a $1 trade. MOONX pays the network (gas) fees on all MOONX transactions, so a small trade doesn't carry a gas cost either.

The spread matters more at small sizes than people expect. You buy at the best ask, which can be a cent above the price on screen. On a 5¢ share, one cent is a fifth of the price.

Does the same minimum apply on MOONX?

Yes. MOONX passes your order to Polymarket, where it's placed and matched under Polymarket's rules, so both floors apply the same way. MOONX's site puts it simply: trades start at just over $1.
